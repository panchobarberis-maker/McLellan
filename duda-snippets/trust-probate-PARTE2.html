Deadlines

Deadlines in Trust and Probate Cases

Deadlines in trust and probate cases are shorter than people expect. If you received a formal notification from a trustee that a trust has become irrevocable, you generally have 120 days from the date that notification was served to bring a contest. That is a short window and it does not extend because you were gathering information or waiting on a family member to decide. Other deadlines in this area run on their own schedules, and some of them start from a date you may not have noticed at the time. If you have received anything on letterhead from a trustee, an executor, or a probate court, treat it as time sensitive and let us look at it.

Conservatorships

Conservatorship Services

When an adult can no longer manage their own finances or personal care, a court can appoint a conservator to do it for them. These cases are contested more often than people expect, usually because family members disagree about who should serve, or because a conservatorship is being sought over the objection of the person it would cover. We handle both sides, including petitions to remove or replace a conservator who is not acting in the conservatee's interest, and objections where a conservatorship is not the least restrictive option available. Learn more about how these matters connect to financial elder abuse on our dedicated page.

Family Disputes

Litigating Against Heirs

Unfortunately, after the death of a loved one, siblings, relatives, or others may challenge how the assets should be divided. These cases are emotional, particularly where one child uses the death of a parent to claim that other relatives have engaged in bad acts towards the parent. Our attorneys have experience handling these cases, even going to trial to protect our clients' interests.

Our Approach

Why These Cases Are Different

These cases are different because of who is on the other side. In most litigation the opposing party is a stranger, a company, or an insurer. In trust and probate disputes it is usually a sibling, a stepparent, or someone else who will still be at the funeral. That changes what a good outcome looks like, and it changes how a case should be run. We take the position that aggression is a tool and not a default. Sometimes the right move is a firm letter and a mediation, sometimes it is a petition filed the same week, and the difference is usually a judgment about the other side rather than about the law. We will give you that read honestly, including when it points away from litigation.

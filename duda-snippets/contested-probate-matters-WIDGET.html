What Is a Contested Probate Matter?

Understanding Contested Probate Matters in California

Probate is meant to be an orderly, court-supervised process, but disagreements often arise long before a will's validity is even questioned.

A contested probate matter can involve disputes over who should serve as executor, how the estate's assets should be valued and divided, whether an executor is properly performing their duties, or claims by creditors and omitted heirs demanding a share of the estate.

These disputes proceed within the probate court under the oversight of a judge.

The judge can:

  • Resolve disagreements over asset valuation.
  • Approve or reject an executor's accounting.
  • Rule on competing claims to specific property.
  • Address allegations that an executor is mismanaging or delaying administration.

Because multiple family members and interests are often involved, contested probate matters can become procedurally complex even without a formal will contest.

Estates can remain tied up for months or years when disputes go unresolved.

That delays distributions and increases legal costs that erode what heirs ultimately receive. Whether you are an executor facing objections to your administration, or an heir who believes the estate is being mishandled, timely legal representation helps move the case toward resolution while protecting your position.

Contested Probate FAQs

A probate matter becomes contested when interested parties disagree over an issue the court must resolve. such as who should serve as executor, whether property was properly valued, whether an executor is fulfilling their duties, or whether an omitted heir or creditor has a valid claim against the estate. Not every contested probate involves a will contest.

Beneficiaries can formally object to an executor's accounting, petition the court to compel information about estate assets, and, in serious cases, seek the executor's removal for mismanagement, self-dealing, or unreasonable delay. Courts take these petitions seriously given the executor's fiduciary obligations to the estate.

Uncontested California probate typically takes nine months to a year and a half. Contested matters can take significantly longer, particularly when they involve extensive discovery, competing appraisals, or multiple hearings, though many disputes resolve well before trial once the parties exchange evidence.

An omitted heir. such as a child born after the will was signed, or a spouse married after the estate plan was created. may have statutory rights to a share of the estate under California's pretermitted heir provisions, even if the will does not name them. Whether those rights apply depends on the specific facts and the language of the estate plan.

Disputes frequently arise when an executor rejects a claim the creditor believes is valid, or approves a claim heirs believe should be contested.

Beneficiaries can petition the probate court to compel a final distribution once the estate's debts, taxes, and expenses have been resolved. If an executor is unreasonably delaying distribution without justification, the court can order the distribution and, in some cases, surcharge the executor for the delay.

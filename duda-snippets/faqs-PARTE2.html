Contract Disputes

A contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties that creates mutual obligations enforceable by law. Contracts can be written or oral and must include an offer, acceptance, consideration, and mutual intent to be bound. In California, contracts must comply with state laws and regulations to be valid.

An oral contract can be legally binding, but it is often harder to prove than a written contract. In California, certain agreements, like those involving real estate or contracts that cannot be performed within a year, must be in writing to be enforceable. Without written documentation, enforcing an oral contract relies on witness testimony and other evidence, so it is advisable to document agreements in writing whenever possible.

The statute of limitations refers to the time limit within which a lawsuit must be filed. In California, it is four years for written contracts and two years for oral contracts, starting from the date of the breach or when the breach should have been discovered. If the lawsuit is not filed within this timeframe, the right to sue may be lost.

Mediation

Mediation is a voluntary dispute resolution process where a neutral third party, the mediator, helps the parties reach a mutually agreeable solution. Unlike litigation or arbitration, mediation focuses on negotiation and collaboration rather than a binding ruling from a judge or arbitrator. In California, mediation is often mandatory before your trial.

Mediation is typically faster, less expensive, and more private than trial, and allows parties to maintain control over the outcome. However, it has disadvantages, such as the lack of a binding decision if no agreement is reached. A trial provides a definitive, legally enforceable judgment but can be time-consuming, costly, and public.

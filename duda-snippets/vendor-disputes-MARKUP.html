What Is a Vendor Conflict?

Understanding Vendor & Supplier Disputes in California

Vendor disputes arise when a supplier, service provider, or business partner fails to meet its contractual obligations.

That can mean late or defective deliveries, SLA violations, substandard work product, or unilateral changes to pricing and terms. These disputes often unfold gradually, as a vendor relationship that once worked smoothly begins to break down under missed deadlines, quality complaints, or disagreements over scope.

Vendor agreements involve ongoing performance, not a single transaction, so disputes can be harder to pinpoint than a one-time breach.

Common flashpoints include:

  • Failure to meet negotiated SLAs or uptime guarantees.
  • Delivery of goods that don't conform to specifications.
  • Improper termination of a vendor contract.
  • Disputes over indemnification, limitation-of-liability, or payment terms buried in a master services agreement.

A vendor problem left unaddressed can cascade into bigger business risk.

It can delay your own deliverables, damage client relationships, and expose your business to downstream liability. Whether you need to enforce an agreement against an underperforming vendor or defend your business against a vendor's claims, prompt legal review of the governing contract is critical to protecting your position.

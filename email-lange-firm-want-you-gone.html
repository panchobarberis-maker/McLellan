Some jobs end with a conversation. Others start to feel like they are ending long before anyone says so. The work changes, the tone changes, and you find yourself wondering whether you are reading too much into it. Nothing was said outright—but something clearly changed.

 

It Usually Starts Small

A project gets reassigned. You stop being copied on emails you used to be part of. Your schedule shifts, or your responsibilities quietly narrow. A manager who used to check in stops checking in. On their own, any one of these is easy to explain away. Together, they start to look like a direction.

 

Nothing Is Ever Said Out Loud

There is rarely a memo. No one tells you that your position is at risk, and there may be nothing in writing at all. That silence is what makes the situation so hard to think about clearly. With nothing concrete to point to, employees tend to assume they must be imagining it—and wait to see what happens next.

 

When It Started Can Matter

Think about what came just before the shift. Did it follow a question about your pay, a request for leave, a medical issue, or a concern you raised about something at work? That sequence is worth paying attention to, and it is worth writing down while you still remember the dates. A short record of what changed and when is far easier to make now than to reconstruct later.

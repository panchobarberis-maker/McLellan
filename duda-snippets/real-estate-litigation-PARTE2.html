Real Estate Litigation FAQs

Common types of real estate disputes include boundary disputes, where property lines are contested, and landlord-tenant disputes, involving issues like lease agreements and eviction. Other frequent disputes involve breach of contract in real estate transactions, such as failure to disclose property defects or incomplete transactions. Easement and right-of-way disagreements, zoning and land use conflicts, and title defects are also prevalent. In California, these disputes often require legal intervention to resolve and protect property rights.

If you are involved in a property boundary dispute, start by reviewing your property deed and any related documents to understand your property's legal boundaries. Hiring a licensed surveyor to conduct a professional survey can provide an accurate assessment of the boundary lines. Attempt to resolve the dispute amicably by discussing the issue with your neighbor, supported by the survey results. If an agreement cannot be reached, consult with a real estate attorney experienced in California property law to explore legal options, such as mediation or litigation.

If a real estate transaction falls through, your legal options include negotiating a new agreement or pursuing mediation to resolve disputes. You may also file a lawsuit for breach of contract to seek damages or specific performance, which requires the other party to complete the transaction as agreed. Reviewing the contract terms and any contingencies is crucial to understanding your rights. Consulting with a real estate attorney experienced in California law can help you determine the best course of action.

If you discover a defect in a property after purchase, first review your purchase agreement and disclosure documents to see if the defect was disclosed. Document the defect with photos and detailed notes. Notify the seller in writing about the defect and request a resolution, such as repairs or compensation. If the seller refuses to address the issue, consult with a real estate attorney experienced in California law to explore legal options, including potential claims for misrepresentation or breach of contract.

Specific performance in real estate litigation is a legal remedy where the court orders a party to fulfill their obligations under a real estate contract. This remedy is often used when monetary damages are insufficient, particularly in unique property transactions. In California, a buyer or seller may seek specific performance to compel the other party to complete the sale as agreed, provided the requesting party satisfies the requirements for equitable relief. To obtain specific performance, the requesting party must generally prove that they have met all contractual requirements and that the property is unique.

Easement and right-of-way disputes are handled by reviewing the property deeds and any recorded easement agreements to determine the legal rights involved. If the terms are unclear, a surveyor may be needed to define the exact boundaries and usage rights. Mediation or negotiation can often resolve these disputes amicably between property owners. If a resolution cannot be reached, litigation may be necessary, where a court will interpret the easement and rule on the dispute.

Title defects, such as liens, encumbrances, or errors in public records, can prevent the clear transfer of property ownership and lead to disputes with third parties. These issues often surface during a real estate transaction and can overlap with the purchase and sale disputes, boundary disputes, and other real estate litigation matters we handle. Title insurance can provide some protection against unforeseen defects, and we can help you understand how a title issue affects a transaction or dispute you are already facing.

Zoning and land use decisions are generally challenged by filing an appeal with the local zoning board or planning commission within the required timeframe, presenting evidence and arguments supporting your position, and, if that is unsuccessful, filing a lawsuit in a California court to seek judicial review. These disputes often overlap with the boundary, easement, and property rights matters we handle, and we can help you understand how a zoning or land use issue fits into a broader real estate dispute you are facing.

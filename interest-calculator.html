How Interest Can Change What a Claim Is Worth

Interest can dramatically affect the value of a claim. California law sometimes allows interest before judgment, called prejudgment interest, and after judgment until payment, called post-judgment interest.

The rules depend on claim type, contract terms, dates, and sometimes court discretion. This tool walks you through the questions needed to generate an educational estimate.

Prejudgment Interest vs Post-Judgment Interest

Interest in California lawsuits can accrue during two phases.

Prejudgment interest may apply before a judgment is entered, when the law allows it.

Post-judgment interest generally accrues after a money judgment is entered and continues until the judgment is paid.

Because the rules depend on claim type, dates, and sometimes court discretion, this tool uses a guided decision tree and only calculates where a mathematical estimate is reasonably appropriate.

What Type of Interest Do You Want to Estimate?

Choose prejudgment interest if you have a claim or lawsuit that has not yet resulted in a court judgment, for example unpaid invoices, breach of contract, or a pending personal injury case.

Choose post-judgment interest if a court has already entered a money judgment and you want to estimate how much interest has accrued since the judgment date.

Prejudgment interest is more complex than post-judgment interest. Whether it applies depends on claim type, whether damages were certain, whether a contract sets an interest rate, and whether the court has discretion. This tool calculates only in narrower situations where a mathematical estimate is appropriate. See Cal. Civ. Code sections 3287 through 3289 and Cal. Code Civ. Proc. section 998(d).

What type of claim is involved?

Prejudgment interest rules vary by claim type. See Cal. Civ. Code section 3287 for liquidated claims and Cal. Civ. Code section 3288 for unliquidated claims.

Understanding California Interest Law

What is prejudgment interest in California? Compensation for the time between when a loss occurred and when a judgment is entered. It is available in some cases and not others.
How post-judgment interest works. Once a money judgment is entered, interest generally accrues on the unpaid balance until the judgment is satisfied.
How courts calculate interest on judgments. The rate, the start date, and how partial payments are applied all affect the total.
Common situations where interest may be owed. Unpaid invoices, breach of a written agreement, and unsatisfied money judgments are the most frequent.

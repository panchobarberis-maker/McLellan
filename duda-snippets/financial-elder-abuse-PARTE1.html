The Basics

What Is Financial Elder Abuse?

Financial elder abuse is a form of exploitation that occurs when someone takes advantage of an elderly person's vulnerability to gain access to their finances or assets. Under California Welfare & Institutions Code § 15610.30, financial abuse can occur when a person or entity takes, obtains, appropriates, retains, or assists in taking an elder's or dependent adult's property for a wrongful use, with intent to defraud, or by undue influence. As California's elders continue to get older, sadly, so do the opportunities for those who would abuse them. Our attorneys have experience protecting elders from bad actors who engage in financial elder abuse. We also protect heirs who have seen their elders suffer at the hands of abusers, and our attorneys have obtained favorable trial results in financial elder abuse matters.

Financial abuse does not always involve outright theft. California law also recognizes abuse accomplished through undue influence, meaning excessive persuasion that overcomes a person's free will and produces an inequitable result. This commonly arises in disputed gifts, beneficiary changes, deeds, and trust amendments.

Common Forms

What Financial Elder Abuse Can Look Like

Financial elder abuse can take many forms, including unauthorized transfers or withdrawals, misuse of a power of attorney, taking title to an elder's home through deception or undue influence, pressuring an elder into gifts or loans, improper beneficiary or trust amendments, and misappropriation by a caregiver, relative, fiduciary, or financial professional, or by someone who assists another person in wrongfully obtaining the elder's assets.
